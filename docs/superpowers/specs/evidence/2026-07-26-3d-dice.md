# 3D dice — evidence pack

Date: 2026-07-26
Branch: `worktree-three-d-dice`
Plan: `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-07-26-3d-dice.md`
Specs: `2026-07-26-3d-dice-design.md`, `2026-07-26-sampled-motion-tracks-design.md`,
`2026-07-26-dice-roll-treatments.md`

The die's flat 2D reel is gone. A solid inferred from the face count is thrown
by a seeded rigid-body simulation, the server's value is painted onto whichever
face the physics turned up, and the trajectory is baked to literal matrices and
played as one sampled curve track on the `visual` channel.

This document is in two parts. The first discharges the **nine hazards** the
sampled-motion design named as binding requirements, each with the test that
holds it. The second records the **defects this branch found that the plan did
not anticipate**, which is the more honest evidence of what the work was.

---

## Part 1 — the nine hazards

The hazards are the nine binding requirements in
`2026-07-26-sampled-motion-tracks-design.md`: the seven sections under
"Constraints the hazard review forced", plus the two in "Required harness
change" (the fingerprint's monotone-travel assumption, and the harness sampling
`frac = 1`).

### H1 — Host sampling is forbidden, in the type

**Hazard.** A sampled `host` track would break three contracts at once: the
FLIP resting write (`boardgame-component.ts:356`), `StructuralMotionPath`'s
two-point viewport contract, and trail-echo synchronization, which animates
echoes along a straight `fromCenter → toCenter` line under the effect easing.
Leaving this to discipline means one future producer breaks all three silently.

**Discharged.** `ComponentMotionTrackInput` is a union whose curve member fixes
`target: 'visual'`, so a host curve does not type-check; the compiler throws as
well, because `boardgame-component-animator.ts:596` feeds ALREADY-COMPILED
tracks back through `componentMotionTracks()`, making the compiler a validator
of untrusted `planMotionTracks()` subclass output rather than only a compiler of
first-party input.

**Held by.** `component-track.test.ts` — *refuses a curve on the host channel*;
*revalidates a compiled track as strictly as it compiles one* (the pass-through
path, which an earlier implementation accepted five ill-formed states through,
including this one).

### H2 — Sampled tracks require `timing: 'immediate'`

**Hazard.** Under the default `'version'` policy `timing.ts:130-141` clamps
duration; offsets being fractional, a 1500ms bake in a 600ms slot plays
uniformly at 2.5× — geometrically faithful, physically absurd, and silent.
Worse, the same policy can resolve to `{kind:'skip'}`, so `play()` returns null,
`playMotionTracks` reports `not-started`, and the animator cancels the batch's
SIBLING tracks. Both are invisible in solo play, where the ambient animation
context is null.

**Discharged.** `boardgame-die` plays its roll with `{ timing: 'immediate' }`.
The test does not merely assert the option is passed — it installs a real
companion `animationContext` with a 600ms slot, which is the condition under
which the bug exists at all.

**Held by.** `die-roll.spec.ts` — *a companion cycle does not clamp the roll
into its slot* (asserts the played duration is the trajectory's own, and that
the trajectory is longer than the slot so the case is not vacuous), and *plays
exactly one gated animation for the trajectory's own duration*.

**Sabotage measured.** Dropping `timing: 'immediate'` fails only 1 of 15
tests — precisely the companion test written for it. That is worth recording:
without a companion context nothing else in the suite can see this hazard, which
is exactly the "only in companion mode" property the design predicted.

### H3 — Validation belongs at the producer boundary, not in a throw

**Hazard.** "Throw loudly" does not exist at runtime here.
`_planMotionTracks` wraps compilation in try/catch, logs and returns host-only
tracks; `playMotionTracks` catches and returns `playback-error`. A throw is
therefore *silence* in the animator path — while in the die's path, called bare
inside `updated()`, the same throw aborts a render pass.

**Discharged, in two of three parts.**
- *Validate in the bake.* `dice-bake.ts` rejects empty sample lists,
  non-monotonic times, non-finite geometry, a non-finite radius, a resting
  orientation disagreeing with the final sample, and a duration shorter than the
  trajectory (which would truncate the roll and break the
  `restingTransform === curve(1)` contract). All pure, all unit-tested.
- *Clamp quality knobs rather than rejecting.* See H7.
- *Surface a real loss as a `motion-invalid` StructuralExecution reason.*
  **NOT DONE — deferred, with reasoning.** Task 2 found that `playAnimation`
  calls `playMotionTracks` unguarded, so a throw strands the host at its
  inverted FLIP transform AND takes out every remaining component in the
  animator's bare loop. This branch adds ZERO reachable instances of that: the
  timing object `playAnimation` builds has no `easing` key, ever, so the one new
  throw H4's sibling introduces cannot fire on that path. It is a pre-existing
  animator robustness defect, recorded in the follow-ups rather than smuggled
  into this branch.

**Held by.** `dice-bake.test.ts` — the seven tests under *trajectoryCurve
validation*; `component-track.test.ts` — *refuses a constant curve instead of
silently vacating the channel*.

### H4 — `will-animate` must fire per gated play

**Hazard.** It used to dispatch only on the 0→1 gated transition. That was
harmless only because every track in a batch shared one timing. Once sampled
tracks carry their own longer duration — and `compileComponentMotionTracks`
orders `visualTracks` LAST — the watchdog would arm from the short host FLIP's
settle time and force-close mid-tumble.

**Discharged.** `will-animate` is now a declaration on every gated play, each
firing carrying that play's own settle budget. `AnimationGate.willAnimate` is
idempotent and monotone by construction (a keyed write plus a strictly
increasing deadline), which is what makes the extra firings safe.

**Held by.** `curve-tracks.spec.ts` — *every gated play declares itself so the
watchdog tracks the longest*; and, end to end in the live renderer,
`die-roll.spec.ts` — *a roll past the watchdog floor extends the deadline
instead of being cut off*.

**Measured, not asserted in the abstract.** A d48 whose throw runs to the
simulator's 5000ms cap, mounted inside the real renderer with the real registry,
the real listener, and a real cycle opened by a real move: with the declaration
reaching the gate, the gate stays open 5532ms, the watchdog does not fire, and
the tumble reaches progress 1.0. With `expectedSettleMs` dropped on the way in,
the gate closes at 4003ms — the bare floor — the watchdog fires once, and the
die is force-settled at 78% of its throw while still `running`. The OTHER
app-level roll test passes under that same sabotage, because a one-second pig
roll never reaches the floor. That is why this blind spot survived until a
scenario long enough to need it existed.

### H5 — The resting-pose contract is the component's

**Hazard.** With `fill: 'none'`, both `finish()` and natural completion render
the **resting style**, not the last sample. `playAnimation` writes resting style
for `host` only; the `visual` channel has no framework write, which is why
`boardgame-card` maintains its inner transform by hand. A curve track that did
not carry its own resting value would snap back to the pre-roll pose the instant
it finished — or be force-settled there by the cycle sweep.

**Discharged.** A track carries `resting`, defaulting to `curve(1)`, and
`playMotionTracks` writes it to the resolved target immediately after starting
the animation. For the die, resting and the final keyframe are produced by the
same formatter behind the same constant scene prefix, so they agree byte for
byte — one rounding digit of disagreement is a visible twitch.

**Held by.** `component-track.test.ts` — *defaults resting to curve(1)*;
`curve-tracks.spec.ts` — *starting a sampled track writes its resting value to
the resolved target*; `dice-bake.test.ts` — *equals curve(1) exactly, so nothing
jumps when the animation is removed*; `die-roll.spec.ts` — *an interrupted roll
still leaves the right face presented*, which calls `finishGatedAnimations()`
mid-flight and then compares the held matrix against the curve's end (`< 1e-3`),
and *runs no tumble and still presents the right face* under reduced motion,
where the effect resolves to duration 0 and the resting write is the ONLY thing
that puts the die in the right pose.

**Sabotage measured.** Setting `resting` to `curve(0.5)` fails 5 tests across
two files.

### H6 — Bake literal matrices

**Hazard.** Chromium composites transform keyframes only when the values are
compositable. Literal `matrix3d(...)` is; `var()`/`calc()` is not, and the old
die used exactly that (`translateY(calc(-1 * var(--effective-die-size) * N))`),
which forces a multi-second tumble onto the main thread.

**Discharged.** `trajectoryCurve` emits literal `matrix3d`, and the component's
scene prefix is a literal `translate3d` in px plus up to two literal `rotate3d`
turns. The die's pixel radius is read from the DOM as a NUMBER and passed to the
bake; interpolating `--effective-die-size` into the matrix string would have
forfeited the guarantee that is the whole point.

**Held by.** `dice-bake.test.ts` — *emits only literal matrix3d values, never
var() or calc()*; `die-roll.spec.ts` — *every keyframe is a literal transform
list, so the tumble composites*, which asserts both the absence of `var(`/`calc(`
and the positive shape of every frame.

**Sabotage measured.** Leaving `radiusPx` at its default of 1 fails 2 tests:
the die still tumbles, still lands on the right face and still squares up — it
just travels 2.5% of its own width instead of more than 60%. That is why the
travel distance is asserted in units of the die's own size.

### H7 — `resolution` clamps and decimates

**Hazard.** Rejecting an over-budget resolution turns a quality knob into a
failure, against the `effect-budget.ts` precedent where `reserveEffectBudget`
returns null to degrade and never throws.

**Discharged.** `resolution` is clamped to `[2, 256]`, default 64, and a
non-numeric value falls back to the default rather than rejecting. A 90-sample
trajectory resampled to N is the same trajectory. The die asks for
`round(durationMs / 16.7)`, one 60Hz frame, so a roll past ~4.3s samples coarser
than a frame — reachable only by the largest barrels, since the simulator caps a
throw at 5s.

**Held by.** `component-track.test.ts` — *clamps resolution rather than
rejecting it*; *clamps a NaN resolution to the default instead of rejecting it*;
`dice-bake.test.ts` — *interpolates at a resolution the samples do not line up
with*, and the seeded *never jumps more than the trajectory's own rate allows*
continuity block.

### H8 — The harness fingerprint assumed monotone travel

**Hazard.** `geometry-helpers.ts` fingerprinted transforms as
`matrixDist(p, first) / matrixDist(last, first)` — normalizing by NET
displacement. Against a tumble that fails four ways: values far past 1 on an
absolute 0.08 tolerance; a near-zero denominator for a trajectory landing near
its start, which either explodes the ratios or nulls the channel outright (and
an all-null curve is DROPPED, so the most complex animation in the app would
contribute nothing, silently); rotation drowned by translation, because a single
Frobenius sum mixes unitless rotation entries with raw pixels and a 60px travel
makes rotation ~5% of the norm; and run-to-run instability, since the landing
orientation changes the denominator.

**Discharged, as a DECLARED fingerprint change** — not as a golden regeneration
to make something pass. Normalization moved to cumulative path length
(`Σ‖pᵢ − pᵢ₋₁‖`), which is monotone non-decreasing and in `[0,1]` by
construction, and the transform channel was split into separate `rotation`
(the matrix's linear part) and `translation` (its translation column) channels
because their scales are incomparable. The protocol was: prove the OLD
fingerprint green on the OLD goldens first; touch only `geometry-helpers.ts`
and the README; require translation-only curves to keep IDENTICAL values or
stop. Trace goldens were verified byte-identical against their four pre-change
SHA-256s; only the 6 geometry goldens moved, and value changes appear only in
rotation-bearing curves — the old `[0, 0.2, 0.71, 0.98, 1]` is exactly
`sin(θ/2)/sin(90°)` for a 180° flip, and now reads the true easing
`[0, 0.13, 0.5, 0.87, 1]`.

**Held by.** `fingerprint-normalization.spec.ts` — *a tumble that returns to its
start pose is not dropped*; *a non-monotone tumble stays in [0,1] and never
decreases*; *rotation is reported separately from pixel-scale translation*; plus
*recorded geometry goldens are well-formed* over the whole corpus.

**Not discharged as designed, and recorded as such.** The design also called for
a die golden recorded from a fixed-value fixture, and for that scenario to
assert its curve is never all-null. No die scenario was added to the geometry
suite. The reasoning and the residual exposure are written into the parity
README's blind-spot ledger: a fingerprint golden is *shape under
normalization*, and the die's shape is a seeded physics trajectory, so the
golden would restate the simulator rather than a product invariant. The die is
pinned instead by `die-roll.spec.ts` asserting RENDERED KEYFRAMES against a
trajectory recomputed in-page from the component's own exported seed derivation,
which is strictly tighter than a 0.08-tolerance curve set. What is genuinely
lost is the die's participation in the cross-cutting curve SET.

**Coverage regression, surfaced honestly and then repaired.** Path-length
normalization is magnitude-invariant by construction, so the documented
flip-magnitude tooth (`rotateY(180)` → `rotateY(90)` must fail memory's reveal)
stopped biting — the old lens was magnitude-sensitive only by ACCIDENT, via the
same shape/magnitude entanglement that made it unusable for a die. Restored with
`sweptRotationDegrees()`: Gram-Schmidt decomposition of each sampled matrix to a
pure rotation, then accumulation of the angle between SUCCESSIVE orientations,
so a multi-turn tumble stays measurable (a 360° roll's start-to-end angle is 0).
`geometry.spec.ts`'s *memory: reveal flip curves* asserts the largest swept
angle is 180° ± 3°. Proven by applying the real sabotage to `boardgame-card.ts`:
the assertion failed at 89.99999999999993, and the curve-SET comparison PASSED
under the same sabotage — the scalar is doing all the work. The restored tooth
is honestly narrower than the original: it pins memory's flip at one call site,
and the README says so.

### H9 — The harness samples `frac = 1`, which records resting style

**Hazard.** `frac = 1` is exactly the after-phase boundary, so the harness
records the element's RESTING STYLE, not the animation's last sample. If those
differ, the golden normalizes against a matrix the animation never reached.

**Discharged.** By making them the same object. `restingTransform(die)` is
byte-identical to `trajectoryCurve(die, …)(1)`, and the component composes the
same constant scene prefix in front of both, so `resting` and the final keyframe
agree to the last digit. `settledTrajectory` — which trims the simulator's dead
trailing hold — deliberately rebuilds its `restingOrientation` from its own
final sample for exactly this reason, so trimming cannot silently open a gap
between the two.

**Held by.** `dice-bake.test.ts` — *equals curve(1) exactly, so nothing jumps
when the animation is removed* and *equals curve(1) for a hand-built trajectory
too*; `die-roll.spec.ts` — *every keyframe is a literal transform list*
(asserts the last frame ends with the bake's own resting transform) and *an
interrupted roll still leaves the right face presented* (measures the held
matrix against the curve's end after a forced settle).

## Part 2 — defects the plan did not anticipate

Every item below was found DURING the work, by review or by running the thing,
and none of them is in the plan.

### Barrel cap facets were stable resting surfaces

The geometry doc claimed a barrel "cannot come to rest on an end". True of the
apex POINT; false of the cone FACETS. Task 4's review had checked that the apex
vertex was the unique z-extreme — a STRUCTURAL check, which cannot see facet
stability. Only running the physics exposed it: cap facets are stable for every
side count ≥ 5, with a d7 support margin of +0.161, and **64% of unretried d7
rolls landed on one, 61° off vertical**. Every face count outside
{3, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 20} was unshippable.

The physics could not fix it; the geometry had to change. The fix was DERIVED,
not tuned: the module now fixes the product `h·c = √3·cos²(π/N)` from the exact
tipping condition `h·c > cos²(π/N)`, with the √3 taken from the one barrel that
was already correct. Raising the cap alone cannot work — with a square band the
required cap grows as N/π, a 48:1 needle at N=100 whose margin collapses to
0.0002 circumradii — so the band is stretched to the length-minimising height
once the square height falls below it, and side faces are rectangles rather than
squares for N ≥ 5. Cap margins are now −0.168 to −0.189 circumradii for all N in
3..200, and 0 of 240 landings are unreadable at every face count.

An independent re-derivation of the tipping condition matched the module's
analytic prediction to 2.6e-15 for 15 face counts up to N=200.

**Consequence nobody would have predicted:** a d7 side face is 2.37 × 0.87, not
square, which became a live trap for the renderer.

### `toScreen` rendered the solid as its own mirror image

Found by end-to-end integration, in Task 10. `toScreen` mapped
`(x, y, z) → (x, −y, −z)`. That is a PROPER rotation (determinant +1) into CSS's
LEFT-handed frame, so it drew the solid's MIRROR IMAGE: the ±z face pair swapped,
and a rolled die presented the face OPPOSITE the one physics turned up. A
handedness change needs a reflection (determinant −1) — flip Y, and only Y.

Evidence: the winding of a right-handed corner against its own z measured
+0.4126 before the fix and −1.0934 after. **Every unit test passed while this was
live**, because the bug is in the physics→CSS boundary that only end-to-end play
exercises. Nothing about a stationary d6 shows it.

Now pinned by `die-roll.spec.ts` — *renders the die the physics threw, not its
mirror image* — which states the invariant physically: three mutually
perpendicular outward normals carrying values 1-2-3 must wind counter-clockwise
seen from outside, so their projected screen winding equals MINUS the corner's
own z. Reverting the fix fails 4 tests.

### The simulation seed silently aliased through a ToInt32

The doc said the seed accepted "any finite number". `createRandom` did
`Math.trunc(seed) ^ 0x9e3779b9`, a ToInt32. Verified identical rolls for the
pairs 3/3.7, 1/2³²+1, −1/−2³²−1, and 0/1e-9. This landed squarely on the
renderer, which derives its seed from state, and the failure mode was **distinct
game states rolling identically with no signal**.

Fixed with a full-double hash: all 64 bits read little-endian through a
`DataView` (a `Uint32Array` view is HOST-endian and would break cross-engine
reproducibility), both halves avalanched through splitmix32, and −0 folded onto
0. All six aliasing pairs now give bitwise different trajectories.

### The energy invariant held only at the default restitution

The suite tested one restitution value. The invariant FAILED at restitution
≥ ~0.85, which the API validated as legal: worst single-step gain 3.8e-2 at
r=0.85 and 2.8e-1 at r=1.0, on a SINGLE die — not a multi-body artifact. The
cause is classic multi-contact restitution pumping: each contact of a
flat-landing die independently demands restitution × its OWN pre-solve approach
speed, and more solver iterations do not help.

Fixed properly rather than clamped: restitution moved out of the iteration loop
into a single post-convergence pass (Poisson's hypothesis), so a manifold bounces
with restitution × the impulse it was JOINTLY solved to need. Two things the
review had not anticipated came out of doing it — Poisson alone measured WORSE
(5.6e-1 at r=0.95), because a penetrating contact's accumulated impulse is the
depenetration BIAS rather than a compression impulse, so it also needs Newton's
rule capped per contact; and gravity is applied BEFORE the solve, so at r=1 a
perfect bounce handed back that step's `g·dt`. Worst per-step gain went 2.8e-1 →
1.95e-5 at r=1 and 8.25e-2 → 2.8e-8 at r=0.85, with the energy delta across the
whole contact solve now exactly 0.00e+0 at r=0.32/0.95/1.0. A restitution sweep
test was added.

### A same-face roll could not animate, because the server sent a byte-identical blob

Task 10 triggered the tumble on `selectedFace`, an INDEX into the face list. A
throw landing on the face already showing leaves that index alone — one throw in
six for a d6, measured at 4 of 20 rolls in pig — so the player clicked Roll and
the die did not move. In pig, a game entirely about rolling, that is very
visible.

The client genuinely could not tell a re-roll from a no-op: a same-face throw
produces a byte-identical `DynamicValues` blob. Alternatives were rejected with
reasons — `selectedFace` IS the bug; the state version moves three times during
one mount with the die untouched; the move's `AnimationKey` names a MOVE, not a
die, and would fire the wrong die in a two-dice game. The fix is a new
server-side `RollCount` on `components/dice`'s `DynamicValue`, incremented by
`Roll()` and by nothing else.

Verified in one process against a rebuilt binary: `RollCount` reaches the client
(0 at mount, not null), 13 real pig rolls observed, all 13 animated, including a
genuine same-face roll (index 0 twice, value 1).

Task 12 then moved the SEED onto the same signal. `(component id, state
version)` was the wrong key for the same reason the trigger was: the version
moves while one throw is on screen, so a component remounting mid-roll would
re-derive a DIFFERENT trajectory for the SAME roll and the die would change its
path in mid-air. Pinned by `die-roll.spec.ts` — *re-derives the same throw for
one roll count at any state version* — which plays one roll count at two
unrelated state versions and requires the two throws to agree keyframe for
keyframe, and additionally ties the played keyframes to a trajectory derived
from the count alone. Reverting the seed turns 4 tests red.

### A third of every roll was a frozen die

Found by filmstrip measurement in Task 11: 3 of 13 frames byte-identical.
`dice-sim.ts` only declares a die at rest after 0.3s of continuous stillness and
it EMITS that hold as samples, so the last ~300ms of every trajectory was the
die sitting perfectly still — and the roll is gated, so that was 30% of a median
roll during which the whole game's animation cycle waited on a die that had
already stopped. `settledTrajectory` cuts it: median −300ms, never changes the
presented face over 1800 throws, and the pose at the cut is within 0.1px of the
end. In-app median went to ~660ms and all 13 frames now differ.

### Tests that passed with AND without the behavior they claimed to check

Six, each caught by sabotage rather than by reading:

1. **A test named "is centred on its centroid" was a verbatim duplicate of a
   different test** and asserted nothing about centering. Volume-centroid
   computation and centering had ZERO coverage — every supported solid is
   centrally symmetric, so the vertex mean equals the volume centroid and ~18
   lines never did work under test. Closing it needed an exported testing seam,
   because no supported face count is asymmetric. Mutation-kill verified:
   centroid → interior gives 2 red, centering removed gives 3 red.

2. **The die-shape spec compared only MULTISETS OF CLIP-PATH VERTEX COUNTS.**
   The reviewer sabotaged `facetStyle` to force every facet box SQUARE —
   precisely the square-facet assumption the barrel fix had made dangerous — and
   ALL 8 TESTS STAYED GREEN while the d7 rendered as disconnected shattered
   slabs and the d6/d12/d20 grew visible seam gaps. Fixed by projecting each
   facet's geometry polygon onto axes read from the browser's OWN resolved
   matrix and requiring it to span the rendered box exactly. Under the sabotage
   4 of 5 extent cases now go red with specific numbers (d7 rendered aspect
   1.000 against a geometry aspect of 2.733; 31.7px box error against a ≤0.05px
   bar). The d6 correctly stays green — a cube's facets really are square.

3. **A `getComputedStyle(#inner)` check for `preserve-3d` could not see the
   property being deleted from `#orient`.** Two independent probes — an
   occlusion-independent perspective A/B measuring a 7.0px projection shift, and
   depth-sorted hit testing — do. The sabotage was reproduced by the reviewer:
   2 failures, with the `getComputedStyle` assertion passing throughout.

4. **The pip-layout suite was blind to where the pips actually were.** The
   brief's own example sabotage — `pipCells` returning N copies of cell [0,0],
   so every dot count is right and all of them are piled in one corner — left
   every count assertion and all six overflow tests green, and was caught only
   by a lattice-position assertion added for it. Separately, an overflow test
   that centred content on the bounding-box centre rather than the polygon
   centroid initially PASSED a sabotage; it was strengthened to measure the
   content square itself and now fails at −12.5px on a d10.

5. **The suite was blind to content ORIENTATION.** Two sabotages went uncaught
   at 34/34: a quarter-turned in-plane `facetBasis` (still orthonormal, still a
   proper rotation) rendering every numeral on every die 90° off, and a content
   region stretched to the facet's bounding-box aspect. The extreme version of
   the second is caught on d10/d12/d20/d4 but NOT on d6/d7 — a rectangle
   stretched inside a rectangular facet never crosses an edge — so the d7
   barrel, the spec's own worked example for the smear, was unprotected.

6. **The one the fix's own author found, unprompted.** Having normalized the
   presented face's roll to upright, the obvious pin is a screen-space
   "the presented numeral is upright" assertion. It does NOT kill the
   basis-quarter-turn sabotage, because the normalizing roll is DERIVED from
   `facetBasis` and absorbs the quarter turn. The killer is a second GEOMETRIC
   assertion: each facet's local +y must be the solid's own down axis projected
   into that facet's plane. Both sabotages now fail (7 red and 6 red
   respectively), and the d7 — the previously unprotected worked example — fails
   first in both.

The same review also measured that the implementer's claim that the presented
face "is upright on every face count I checked" was FALSE for four of seven
shapes (d4 −122°, d10 −116°, d8 −120°, d7 91°). In the RESTING pose — the pose a
die that has never been thrown is shown in, which is the only pose that claim or
its test ever covered — it is now within 0.0005° for all of
d4/d6/d7/d8/d10/d12/d20 at every selected face.

**That sentence is about the resting pose and about content roll, and nothing
else.** It says nothing about a die that has just been thrown, and it says
nothing about legibility. The final whole-branch review found both gaps in the
same place. The post-roll pose was produced by a second, independent routine
that left the presented facet 35° off the camera axis and leaning the opposite
way from the resting pose's 22.4° — a 51.7° disagreement — and post-roll content
roll is deliberately NOT normalized (a real die stops where it stops), so the
"upright" measurement does not even apply there. What does apply is whether the
face carrying the value is the one that READS, and it was not: measured over 25
seeded rolls per shape, counting rolls where some other facet was more square-on
to the camera than the presented one,

| shape | before | worst deficit | after |
|-------|--------|---------------|-------|
| d6    |  0/25  |  —            | 0/25  |
| d10   | 12/25  | 18.3°         | 0/25  |
| d20   | 24/25  | 28.0°         | 0/25  |

and, rendered at 300px, 22 of 25 d20 rolls put a NEIGHBOURING numeral both
bigger on screen (the presented face down to 82.5% of the winner's projected
area) and nearer the centre of the die than the number the die was announcing.
After the fix the presented facet is the largest and the most central on all 25,
by at least 2.8% of area. The two poses are now one routine (`readingPose`),
which also fixed a smaller latent case of the same bug in the resting pose
itself: 8 of a d20's 20 faces rested with a neighbour up to 2.9° more square-on,
because 22.4° of lean is more than half the 41.8° between a d20's facet normals.
